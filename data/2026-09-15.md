Abstract: Global climate finance is entering a critical transition from scaling financial flows toward improving risk identification, pricing, and capital-allocation efficiency. Despite the rapid expansion of climate-related investment, a substantial gap remains between available financing and long-term climate-investment needs. At the same time, climate-finance decisions are increasingly shaped by interacting physical, transition, policy, project-level, systemic, and information risks. Existing research has generated important advances in climate-risk measurement, asset pricing, sustainable finance, climate disclosure, and taxonomy-based classification, but these strands remain only partially integrated. In particular, there is still a need for a unified and empirically testable framework that can capture not only the intensity of multiple climate-finance dimensions, but also their cross-dimensional coherence and its potential implications for risk pricing and capital allocation. This paper develops such a framework within the Prim-Lex (Economic Climatology) theoretical system. It integrates three conceptual layers— “ Five Prim-Fires, Eight-Dimensional Coherence, and Nine-Pivot Guidance ” —into a climate-finance decision architecture. The Five Prim-Fires provide a cross-scale systems perspective; at the project level, however, only the Physical Prim-Fire is currently operationalized quantitatively, while the other four Prim-Fires remain conceptual constructs rather than direct numerical inputs. Building on this architecture, the paper develops the “ PL-8D36 indicator system ” , comprising 36 operational indicators across eight dimensions of climate-finance performance and risk, and introduces the “ Prim-Lex Eight-Dimensional Coherence Index (PICI) ” . Unlike conventional additive climate or ESG scores, PICI is designed to distinguish multidimensional coherence and misalignment from the average intensity of individual dimensions and to test whether coherence contains incremental information beyond conventional risk characteristics. The framework further links PICI to climate-risk pricing, financing-cost adjustment, and capital-allocation decisions. To facilitate institutional application, the paper establishes a mapping between PL-8D36/PICI and major international climate-finance frameworks, including Multilateral Development Bank (MDB) Paris Alignment approaches, the EU Taxonomy, and relevant ICMA principles and guidance. It proposes a decision architecture in which “ hard eligibility and regulatory constraints take precedence over coherence scores ” , while coherence-based assessment provides additional information for risk differentiation, pricing, and portfolio allocation. The framework is illustrated through proposed application settings involving Green Climate Fund (GCF) investments in Central Asia, climate-finance and adaptation activities of the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB), and China's climate-finance pilot programs. The paper formulates twelve testable hypotheses (H1–H12) and specifies an empirical strategy based on project-level panel data, predictive modeling, threshold estimation, block bootstrap procedures, and out-of-sample validation. Importantly, the critical phase threshold is “ not imposed ex ante as a universal constant ” . Instead, it is to be estimated from predictive performance, with 45° treated only as a falsifiable theoretical benchmark. The historical backtesting results currently presented in the paper are simulation and calibration exercises rather than completed real-data, out-of-sample empirical tests. Accordingly, the paper does not interpret the proposed relationships between PICI, climate risk, financing costs, or capital allocation as established causal effects. The contribution of the paper is therefore primarily methodological and architectural: it provides a potentially “ quantifiable, programmable, auditable, and empirically falsifiable framework ” for integrating multidimensional climate conditions, cross-dimensional coherence, information quality, risk pricing, and capital allocation. The framework is intended to complement, rather than replace, existing climate-risk, sustainable-finance, and taxonomy-based approaches, and to provide a testable foundation for moving climate finance from a focus on the “ scale of capital mobilization toward the efficiency and precision of capital allocation ”. The neologism “Prim‑Lex” combines “prim” (fundamental, original principle) and “lex” (rule/law), corresponding to the Chinese term Yuan‑Lü (元律,fundamental law).
